# A Rule Outranks Precedent

A principle, not a gate. Convention governs where no written rule speaks.
Where one does, the rule wins and the precedent is a second violation rather
than a defence — a pattern's presence on the default branch says it shipped,
not that it is permitted. Precedent records what someone did; a rule records
what is permitted, and the gap between them is exactly the debt a rule exists
to stop growing.

## What it rules out

- **"This already exists elsewhere" as a refutation.** More instances of a
  violation are more of the debt, not evidence the rule does not apply.
- **Treating age as authority.** A pattern that has survived for years has
  been unexamined for years, which is the same observation.
- **Reading the tree instead of the rule.** Where a written rule exists, the
  rule is the text to read; sampling the codebase reports what happened, not
  what is allowed.
- **Rewriting the rule to match the tree.** A rule and a body of practice that
  disagree is a finding to report, and taking the practice as the new rule
  quietly settles a question nobody was asked.

## Boundary

- It applies only where a written rule exists. Where none does, convention
  governs, and following what the surrounding code already does is right.
  `nested-agents` states the order: follow convention where no rule speaks,
  and follow the rule where one does.
- Two things do refute a rule-violation claim — the rule does not say what the
  claim says, or the case falls under an exemption the rule itself states.
  Both are readings of the rule, not of the precedent.
- It authorizes nobody to act alone on a genuine rule-versus-convention
  conflict. That conflict is a finding for the report.
